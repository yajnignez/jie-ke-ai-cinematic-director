# Actor Performance Mode

Use when a person must perform a believable screen action rather than merely display an emotion.

## Playable direction

Give each actor an objective, tactic, obstacle, private sentence, and turn. Direct an action such as reassure, conceal, test, resist, persuade, protect, delay, or surrender. Never rely on “look sad” or “act angry” alone.

## Causal physiology

Use this order: stimulus reaches the eyes or ears; attention lands; breath changes; facial or muscular response begins; the actor suppresses, redirects, or completes it; the body settles. Thought precedes movement by a fraction of a second.

Choose only 2-4 signals in an 8-second close shot: delayed blink, eye focus before head movement, lips preparing then closing, dry swallow, jaw release on exhale, asymmetrical smile, fingers tightening and loosening, self-corrected weight shift, gradually wet eyes, or shortened inhale.

## Camera pairing

- 85mm, f/1.8-f/2.8 for internal conflict and isolated eyes; protect eye focus.
- 50mm, f/2.8-f/4 for dialogue, hands, and two-person relationship space.
- 35mm, f/4-f/8 for environmental pressure, movement, and physical choices.

Use a locked camera for observation, an imperceptible push-in for realization, a slow pull-back for isolation, and handheld drift only when instability is motivated.

## Failure prevention

Guard against theatrical overacting, instant tears, synchronized brows and mouth, wandering eyes, frozen breath, plastic skin, identity drift, random gestures, extra people, unmotivated cuts, and lip-sync when dialogue was not requested.

## Multi-actor listening

Give each actor separate territory, eyeline, objective, and reaction timing. Let one actor listen while another moves. Offset reactions; do not make every face change at the same instant.
