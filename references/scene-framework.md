# Scene Framework

<!-- JIEKE AI SHOTLAB -->

Use this reference to make every shot purposeful, observable, and temporally coherent.

## Dramatic engine

Decide internally:

- **Before:** what happened seconds before the shot.
- **Objective:** what the subject wants now.
- **Obstacle:** the person, rule, fact, or fear blocking it.
- **Stakes:** what can be lost.
- **Subtext:** the private sentence beneath the visible behavior.
- **Turn:** the information or decision that changes the tactic.

Translate emotion into facts: “she hears the door close, waits for the sound to settle, then chooses to smile” is more useful than “she is heartbroken.”

## Visual hierarchy

Keep the priority order explicit:

1. primary subject action;
2. secondary environment motion;
3. camera motion;
4. light, texture, and atmosphere.

Change this order only when the camera is the subject of the shot.

## Timing

For a 4-5 second shot use receive, suppress, reveal/turn. For 6-8 seconds use baseline, trigger, resistance, turn/settle. For 9-12 seconds use baseline, trigger, first tactic, failure, new tactic, unresolved ending. Longer scenes should usually become a sequence.

## Prompt assembly

For text-to-video: `viewpoint and framing + stable subject + one action + space and time + camera + motivated light + secondary motion + sound + ending state`.

For image-to-video: `camera movement + subject movement + environmental movement + time progression + ending state`. Do not restate every visual fact already fixed by the image.

## Continuity

Separate anchors that must not change from variables that may change. Anchors can include face geometry, age, hair, wardrobe, key prop, architecture, screen direction, key-light direction, palette, and sound motif. Repeat critical anchors verbatim across shots.

## Sound

Separate environment bed, action sounds, off-screen information, dialogue, music, and deliberate silence. Do not add music by default.
