## Framing and point of view

- Establishing shot: scale, geography, and isolation.
- Medium or medium-wide: body blocking and relationship to space.
- Close shot: a decision, reaction, or small change.
- Low angle: power or pressure; high angle: vulnerability or pattern; eye level: neutral proximity; canted angle: instability.

## Camera movement

Use one primary movement per short shot. Write its path, speed, motivation, starting composition, and ending composition. Distinguish a track from a zoom: a track changes perspective; a zoom changes optical magnification.

## Optics

| Need | Lens | Aperture | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Isolated internal conflict | 85mm | f/1.8-f/2.8 | compressed background, eyes isolated |
| Dialogue and hands | 50mm | f/2.8-f/4 | readable eyelines and moderate separation |
| Architecture and action | 35mm | f/4-f/8 | readable space, balance, and motion |

Use aperture only with a stated visual consequence. Keep eyes or the action plane in focus.

## Light and color

Name the source first: window overcast, sunset, practical lamp, fire, neon, vehicle light, or screen. Then specify direction, softness, intensity, and temperature relationship. Use one dominant color rule and one contrast rule.

## Diagnosis

| Failure | Likely cause | Smallest next change |
|---|---|---|
| Subject drifts | too many identity changes | freeze 3-5 anchors and repeat verbatim |
| Action is weak | abstract verb or no endpoint | add direction, speed, force, and result |
| Camera ignores instruction | multiple competing movements | keep one primary movement |
| Unwanted cuts | multiple locations or events | split into shots and state single continuous take |
| Flicker or morphing | source defect or excessive motion | repair source or reduce motion |
| Emotion feels wrong | emotion not translated into behavior | change viewpoint, timing, or one physical action |
